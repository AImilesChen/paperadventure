# C08 · The Mixed-Up Exhibition · classroom paper game

This is the current C08 retail content contract. `scripts/museum_classroom_exhibition.py` is the authoritative A4/Letter source. The museum object in `retail-games-v1.json` is retained for catalog identity and the general build loop; its old candidate-card stages are superseded.

## First-use promise

One teacher can prepare a complete set from the PDFs, then guide a 2–6-pupil crew without inventing clues or props. For 35 pupils, use seven independent five-person crews. Print 11 Player pages per crew and keep one 10-page Teacher Guide. Required supplies are ordinary paper, a pencil, an eraser, a table and adult-handled scissors. A low-cut path keeps case/part sheets whole for pointing or writing but still cuts and separately deals three private slips.

The fictional Wind Listener makes no real historic, cultural, mechanical or scientific claim. The final object is flat paper; no glue, stand, specialist equipment or purchased prize is needed.

## Mission and answer chain

| Mission | Player action | Props released | Checked result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · accession | Compare two marks with three ledger records | Page 3 | M-27: two arcs and one star |
| 2 · gallery | Place case signs from entrance to window | Page 4; Case 4, 2, 7 cut signs | Case 2 → Case 7 → Case 4; object in Case 4 |
| 3 · conservation | Hear three separately held observations and assemble a model | Pages 5–7; six cut parts; three separately dealt slips | Crescent top, double-ring dial, three-dot base |
| 4 · archive | Reject an unrelated accession, then write an original label | Pages 8–9; M-27/M-72/M-27 sources, blank label | Name Wind Listener; a selected visible feature; imagined breeze purpose |
| 5 · exhibition | Copy the verified accession ID and case number onto the mat, mount the paper object and authored label, then invite an evidence question | Pages 10–11; exhibit mat and audit | Four checks pass; one cited answer; repair/recheck if needed |

The first mission is the only short candidate comparison. Mission 2 is spatial placement, 3 is distributed-evidence assembly, 4 is source appraisal and original writing, and 5 is peer review. A second crew reviews the display, or the teacher acts as visitor for a lone crew. Each teacher mission page has read-aloud story, exact handout, actions, accepted evidence, three hints and handover.

## Print inventory

- Player p1: one cover illustration, story and quick start. The artwork does not repeat in the guide.
- Player p2: four optional job-card cuts and a persistent five-result record.
- Player p3: intact intake task and ledger.
- Player p4: intact gallery plan with three required case-sign cuts. Each cut sign is 18 pt narrower than its placement slot, allowing 9 pt clearance on either side.
- Player p5: neutral TOP/DIAL/BASE board; no private answer printed.
- Player p6: six required part cuts; word and drawing stay inside each border.
- Player p7: three required private slips, cut and dealt separately. With two pupils, the teacher holds slip 3 until the pupils report slips 1 and 2, then reads that clue aloud to both pupils.
- Player p8–9: source task and blank visitor label.
- Player p10–11: flat display mat with separate accession-ID and case-number frames, visitor audit and ending.
- Teacher p1–3: quick start, exact pile inventory, roles and opening; p4–8: five mission scripts; p9: answer atlas; p10: recovery and closing.

Full-cut preparation is **12 required loose pieces per crew** (3 case signs + 6 object parts + 3 private slips); the four job cards are optional cuts. With seven crews, print 77 Player sheets and prepare 84 required loose pieces. Keep later pages and the Teacher Guide away from pupils until their mission.

The A4 and US Letter editions carry the same tasks and answers. Screen rendering, text extraction, bounds checks and ZIP integrity are automated by `scripts/check_museum_classroom_exhibition.py` and `scripts/check_retail_packs.py`. These checks do not establish physical 100%-scale printer behavior or unfamiliar-teacher classroom usability.
