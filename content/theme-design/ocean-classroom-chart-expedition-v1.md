# C07: The Coral Chart Expedition — paper chart edition

This is the current Ocean Classroom game contract. The earlier station-card /
passage-choice / record-order / candidate-report puzzle in
`retail-games-v1.json` is superseded for C07. The ocean PDF source is
`scripts/ocean_classroom_chart.py`.

## Promise and play boundary

The research ship's fictional survey chart has been scattered. A crew of 2–6
students rebuilds it, frames the one unfinished square, combines three
separately held observations, corrects a copied log, and writes its own checked
captain's report. An adult guides each group. Up to 35 pupils can play in
independent groups with a complete Player Pack per group. Everything happens
at a table. Marine facts come only from the game's fictional printouts.

## Answer and physical dependency chain

1. Four upright map quarters fit by four different paired seam marks. The
   north-arrow quarter is upper left. The complete chart has Kelp Bay at A1,
   Lighthouse at B1, Channel at A2, and a neutral-labelled Coral Reef at B2.
   Mission 1 checks the four seams and north arrow only; it does not ask for
   the survey target or name the reef as unfinished.
2. The captain's two references are one square **south of Lighthouse** and one
   square **east of Channel**. Their common square is **B2**. Place the paper
   survey frame around that cell and record B2 before releasing the sightings.
   This is the first point at which the crew learns that Coral Reef is the
   survey target.
3. Three privately dealt observations put a **fan mark in the centre**, **three
   fish marks left of the fan**, and a **striped buoy right of the fan**. A
   supplies the fish count without its side; B supplies the central fan and
   fish side; C supplies the buoy and its side. All three are needed to finish
   the drawing. The Player task page must not print the combined answer.
4. The copied log correctly says B2 but wrongly says *two* fish and *buoy on
   the left*. The crew checks all three entries without being told the error
   count or letters, preserves the original wording, corrects both errors,
   and cites the original printouts. Entry A is checked against the chart and
   captain's two notes; B uses observation slips A and B; C uses slip C. The
   teacher's read-aloud introduction must not name the faulty details.
5. The crew writes its own captain's report with B2, the three plotted
   features, and two justified corrections. The Player report uses neutral
   correction labels rather than naming the fish and buoy answers. No
   pre-completed report candidates.

Each release has a short player-facing scene and a longer adult read-aloud
script. The five actions are physically distinct: assemble, position a frame,
plot shared observations, correct a document, author and verify a report.

## Printable inventory and staging

The Player Pack has nine pages in A4 and Letter: (1) opening, (2) jobs and
running record, (3) four map quarters, (4) chart board / mission 1, (5) survey
frame / mission 2, (6) three separate observation slips, (7) survey plotting
sheet / mission 3, (8) copied log / mission 4, (9) captain's report / mission
5. The Teacher Guide has nine pages: quick start, cutting/group assignment,
five mission scripts, answer atlas, and recovery/ending.

Pre-cut exactly four chart quarters, three private observation slips and one
survey frame per group: eight loose pieces, or 56 for seven five-pupil crews.
The four optional job cards can stay on page 2 and be
pointed to. Adult scissors only. Keep the clue strips separate; do not hand
page 6 intact to students. The frame's inner opening can be cut by an adult;
for a low-cut fallback, children point to the square selected by both position
notes and record its grid name.
Print each group a full Player Pack at Actual Size, single-sided. One guide
copy may be read on screen. Bring ordinary pencils, an eraser, and a table;
no glue, transparent film, pins, devices, prizes, outside marine knowledge,
or student personal details.

## Jobs and classroom variations

Chart Maker assembles and marks the map. Survey Observer positions the frame
and shares held clues. Log Keeper records and corrects. Evidence Checker
compares every final claim with the original printout. For two students,
combine Maker+Observer and Keeper+Checker and swap after missions. For three,
combine Keeper+Checker. For five/six, split Maker or Observer into an operator
and a verifier so every pupil speaks or moves a prop. Rotate physical actions
after each mission. The adult releases the next materials only after the crew
shows the current result and its evidence.

## Production acceptance

- One unfamiliar teacher can prepare, distribute, run and rescue the game
  using only the two PDFs, without inventing lines, props, or answers.
- Both a two-pupil and a six-pupil crew can complete every action; seven
  five-pupil crews can run independently for a class of 35.
- A4 and Letter have their own layout, legible body and card type, complete
  labels, visible cut edges, no clipped text, and useful writing space.
- Player pages never expose later mission answers or all three private
  observations at once; the first chart check cannot disclose the mission 2
  target, and the copied-log task cannot state how many errors exist. Teacher
  answers are held back.
- Screen/PDF checks do not substitute for a physical colour/grayscale print
  and unfamiliar-classroom playtest. Do not claim those tests happened.
