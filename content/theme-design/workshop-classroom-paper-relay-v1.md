# C11 / The Great Blueprint Relay / paper relay edition

This is the current fixed Workshop Classroom design and prop contract.
The former P7/Frame B/Kit 2/F-A-T/Board A candidate-card chain in
retail-games-v1.json is superseded for C11. Preserve the workshop/classroom
URL, ready-made SKU, versioned entitlement identity and old-order access.

## What a crew actually makes

A crew of 2-6 pupils reconstructs three blueprint fragments, folds a one-piece
paper channel, creates a 40 mm-wide message card from three separately dealt
notes, physically tries three sample cards and its own card, documents causes
of failure, then performs a peer-checked display. Up to 35 pupils use complete
sets for balanced crews; no clue must be borrowed from another crew.

The channel is 170 mm long and 78 mm wide before folding: 15 mm left rail,
48 mm clear lane and 15 mm right rail. The dashed outer edge is cut; both
outer rails fold UP on dotted inner lines, which are not cut into slits. The plain calibration card and final blank
message card are 40 mm wide, leaving 8 mm total lane clearance. Sample A is
56 mm wide and cannot pass the clear lane without bending; B is 40 mm wide but
has a reverse arrow; C is 40 mm wide with a forward arrow. Every card is tried
from START with its printed face up and labelled DISPLAY EDGE leading; no sample
may be rotated, flipped or bent to change its result. The three sample cards
are marked TEST ONLY. The hand-made OUR MESSAGE card is the only final display
card, even though Sample C demonstrates the passing condition. Ordinary paper may
need a child's fingertip to hold a leaning rail, which is permitted. No tape,
glue, fastener, powered component or bought item is needed. Print at Actual
size on A4 or US Letter; the two editions preserve these physical dimensions.

| Mission | Story action | Evidence and adult acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rebuild three shuffled blueprint pieces by matching seam words. | X-Y-Z, START to DISPLAY, 15/48/15 mm; CIRCLE and TRIANGLE seams match. |
| 2 | Adult cuts the dashed outer track edge and calibration card; pupils fold rails on the dotted inner lines and slide the plain card. | The 40 mm card travels without bending; rail creases follow both dotted lines. |
| 3 | Three different pupils hold symbol, count and direction notes; a pair's teacher reads the third note after both share. | One hand-made card has a large triangle, exactly two stars below and an arrow toward DISPLAY. |
| 4 | Predict, physically test and diagnose three samples; test the hand-made card. | A too wide, B direction reversed, C passes; the crew documents fit, orientation and a possible repair for one faulty sample without being told its letter in advance. |
| 5 | Demonstrate and peer-check the finished relay. | A Checker other than Recorder points to blueprint, notes, trial record, proposed repair and working card before signing. |

The note labels are information sources, not extra job titles. The only six
crew jobs are Scene Reader, Builder, Tester, Recorder, Checker and Presenter.
The Teacher Guide assigns bundles for 2-6 pupils and rotates them after each
mission. Pupils may point or dictate. The adult pre-cuts; children join, fold,
draw, slide, test, revise and present. No child must operate scissors.
For three pupils, Reader+Presenter, Builder+Recorder and Tester+Checker go to
three different children; the final Checker is never the Recorder. Every Guide
mission assigns an action to all six jobs, whether jobs are bundled or held
individually. For a 35-pupil class, use six self-contained crews (five of six,
one of five); read each scene to everyone, let crews work concurrently, then
release the next pile only once every crew has shown its evidence. Early finishers
help with an explanation rather than taking the next pile. The Guide offers a
provisional 50-minute planning rhythm and explicitly does not claim measured
completion time.

## Exact print inventory

One Player Pack per crew, 12 pages: 1 cover/quick start, 2 jobs, 3 design log,
4 Mission 1 activity, 5 three blueprint pieces, 6 Mission 2 fold directions,
7 one track plus one calibration card, 8 three design notes, 9 one blank final
message card, 10 three physical trial cards, 11 trial/repair record, 12 final
peer-check and display. Full-cut preparation is 12 large pieces:
3 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 3. Mission 1 can use a no-cut written-order option.
One Teacher Guide is 10 pages: quick start, exact cut/handout map, jobs/opening,
five mission scripts (each read-aloud, actions, accepted result, three hints,
failure recovery and handover), answer/geometry atlas, closing/reset.

The illustration appears only on Player page 1. Later pages are actual
printable tools and writing surfaces. Player-facing pages do not state the
assembled piece order or the adult A/B/C trial diagnosis. Answers remain in
the Teacher Guide. This is a fixed paper game, not a real powered mechanism or
an endlessly variable invention.

The first-use repair makes the trial record and finale agree: pupils write one
faulty sample's letter, what failed and how a newly made card could avoid that
fault, even when their own card worked on its first trial. The record offers
no A/B-only choice before testing. The player cover and guide identify ages
6-12. The final check accepts a proposed repair, not a forced modification to
the printed TEST ONLY samples.

Automated extraction, dimensions, rendering and ZIP hash checks do not prove
physical folding clearance or unfamiliar-teacher classroom usability. Do not
claim those observations until a real A4/Letter print and first-use trial.
