# Halloween Home H05 - Paper theatre edition

`The Missing Pumpkin Light` remains a five-mission cooperative paper theatre, separate from Classroom C05. The former four-seal route/parcel/decoder game is superseded for Home. Preserve H05 identity and existing order access when releasing a replacement.

## Player promise and fixed game

Children aged 6-12 inspect eight festival posters for four matching torn edges, fold and cut the four paper actors, share private clues to place them on a two-by-two stage, rehearse through four hinged windows, then perform in a new order over Pip's held-back glowing pumpkin picture. The core props are unchanged: four jobs, one viewer, four silhouettes, four private stage clues and four clue-only cue cards. The stage mat, hinged facade and finale scene remain whole. No real lamp, candle, glue, specialist toy or digital game step is required.

1. All eight posters have borders. Only BAT, OWL, PUMPKIN and STAR have three zigzag tears at their top-right corners. The cut-out paper viewer isolates each edge for comparison. The other four drawings are scenery.
2. The adult pre-cuts only the four outer template cards. In mission 2 each child folds a card, ink out; an adult cuts the bold visible half-shape through both layers. The four unfolded silhouettes become the stage figures.
3. Four separately spoken relations produce exactly one two-by-two arrangement: OWL top left, BAT top right, PUMPKIN bottom left, STAR bottom right. The printed facade does not display actor answers before rehearsal.
4. The adult pre-cuts three long-dashed edges per window, leaving the labelled outer FOLD hinge attached. The window sheet aligns with the mat by four small corner crosses. Cue cards describe actors but do not print their names or window numbers. The Guide's exact four-line rehearsal script uses NIGHT SKY, OLD TREE, WARM PORCH and FINAL SPARK in BAT, OWL, PUMPKIN, STAR order. Players name the actor, recall its stage place and open the matching window.
5. The adult gives the held-back finale scene only after rehearsal. The same four actors transfer to marked circles. The Guide's new finale script uses OWL, PUMPKIN, BAT, STAR order. Four lifted windows reveal a single pumpkin lantern illustration, legible in colour or greyscale. There is no additional code, seal or route puzzle.

First-user corrections: page 5 now prints the three-tear comparison example it names, and the cat poster has whiskers while the owl and bat carry distinct face details. The Stage Checker writes or dictates the record; no undefined Recorder job remains. Mission 4 deals its four cue cards 2/2 for two players, 2/1/1 for three and one each for four. For five or six, extra children point to the stage place or mark the record; cue holders change for the finale. The adult asks players to look away, sets the final scene and actors beneath the *closed* theatre, then invites them back so the lantern is discovered through the windows. Each mission has its own discussion prompt, including an ending question with no nonexistent next scene.

## First-use preparation and group handling

Print a complete 15-page single-sided Player Pack at 100%/Actual size for each team, in A4 or US Letter. A 10-page Guide may stay on screen. Prepare one set for 2-6 players or two complete sets for 7-12. For each team, provide ordinary paper, two pencils, an eraser and adult-handled scissors. Before play cut job cards, viewer opening, private clues and cue cards; cut only the outer borders of the four silhouette template cards; pre-cut and crease the four theatre windows. Keep page 15 hidden until mission 5. The 17 loose pieces count includes four silhouettes made during the game.

Rotate Reader, Maker, Caller and Checker jobs after every mission. Two children combine jobs; five or six pair an active job and alternate the physical action. For two teams, the host checks team A then team B at every mission before releasing the next pages; each team performs its own ending. Spoken answers and adult recording are allowed for early readers.
